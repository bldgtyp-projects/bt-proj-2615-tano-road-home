The **Phius REVIVE** protocol suggests that during the simulation test-period the [Heat Index](https://bigladdersoftware.com/epx/docs/22-2/engineering-reference/resilience-metrics.html#heat-index) temperature should be 
used to evaluate the overall perceived thermal habitability of the zone. In the results shown below, the Heat Index 
for each zone is plotted for the entire test-period summer week. Minimum compliance with the **Phius REVIVE** standard 
requires that the building demonstrate:

- 0-hours of **Heat Index** in the ‘Extreme Danger’ zone ( > 52 °C [125 °F]).

<div class="btwr-callout">
  <strong>Result</strong>
  <span>
The home passes this threshold with a very large margin. Across the full seven-day outage the peak Heat Index in any 
zone reaches only 26.8 °C [80.3 °F]: zero hours in the ‘Extreme Danger’ zone, zero hours in the ‘Danger’ zone, and 
zero hours in the ‘Warning’ zone. The peak only just brushes the bottom of the ‘Caution’ band, and does so for a 
handful of afternoon hours. This occurs even though the outdoor air peaks at 38.4 °C [101.1 °F] during the event. 
The well-insulated, airtight envelope, the low outdoor humidity, and the large Santa Fe day-to-night temperature 
swing together keep the interior dry-bulb temperature at or below 26.7 °C [80.1 °F] for the whole outage. Nights 
fall to roughly 11 °C [52 °F], which lets the house be flushed and re-cooled each night.
  </span>
