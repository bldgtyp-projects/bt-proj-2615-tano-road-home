Below we have included recommendations for the building envelope assemblies based on the energy model results shown in
previous sections. These are recommendations only and should be reviewed by the design team to ensure they are
appropriate for the project.

All R-values quoted here are **effective whole-assembly** values as modeled, meaning they account for framing
fraction, fasteners, and the other thermal bridges internal to the assembly. They run lower than the nominal R-value of
the insulation alone, and they are the numbers that drive the energy results. The assemblies modeled in each variant
are:

| Assembly | Code-Minimum | B.Public base | **+R-52 Walls (recommended)** | Full B.Public | Phius CORE 2024 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exterior wall | R-22.2 | R-33.5 | **R-49.5** | R-49.5 | R-62.1 |
| Exterior roof | R-38.5 | R-58.5 | **R-58.5** | R-75.8 | R-75.8 |
| Ground floor / slab | R-10.0 | R-25.0 | **R-25.0** | R-25.0 | R-41.3 |
| Windows (installed) | U-0.32 | U-0.22 | **U-0.22** | U-0.22 | U-0.15 |

The recommended column is the last point on this table where added insulation still pays for itself: the wall upgrade
that defines it returns 2.0 kBtu/ft²·yr, while the roof upgrade in the next column returns 0.6. The step from there to
the Phius CORE column is where the cost curve turns steep, adding a second, site-applied layer of continuous exterior
insulation over an already-complete panel, roughly doubling the sub-slab foam, and moving the windows to a
considerably more expensive product, all for about 6.6 kBtu/ft²·yr. Note the window row is unchanged across all three
B.Public packages: at U-0.22 they already meet the Passive House comfort recommendation for this climate.

The following assembly U-values have been assessed using project-specific energy-model inputs and supporting
thermal-bridge assumptions. Assembly diagrams should be treated as performance targets and coordination references, not
final construction documents.
