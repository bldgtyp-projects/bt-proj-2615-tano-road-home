This report evaluates the energy performance, thermal comfort, and long-term durability of the proposed
Tano Road home under six design variants, ranging from a code-minimum baseline through the
**B.Public** panel packages to a full **Phius CORE 2024** configuration. The following pages document the
modeling assumptions, the results, and our recommendations.

### What the model shows

The B.Public panel system does most of the work. Moving from the code-minimum baseline to the base
**R-35 wall / R-59 roof** package cuts total site energy roughly in half, from 51.8 to 26.9 kBtu/ft²·yr.
Upgrading to the **R-52 walls** and then the **R-80 roof** takes that to 24.3 kBtu/ft²·yr, and drops annual
CO<sub>2e</sub> emissions from 13.5 to 5.8 tons/year. That is a genuinely excellent building: comfortable,
durable, quiet, and cheap to run.

The full **Phius CORE 2024** variant goes further still, reaching 18.0 kBtu/ft²·yr and 4.2 tons
CO<sub>2e</sub>/year. That sits comfortably inside the 5-ton benchmark described in the
[CO2 Emissions](/energy_model/#co2-emissions) section. It gets there with R-62 effective walls, an R-41
sub-slab assembly, U-0.15 windows, 0.6 ACH50 airtightness, and a shortened, heavily insulated ventilation
duct run.

### Where certification stands

Of the four Phius CORE 2024 space-conditioning criteria, the Phius variant passes three. It meets the peak
heating load limit (5.6 against 5.7 Btu/hr·ft²) and clears both cooling criteria by a wide margin. It falls
short on annual heating demand: **9.45 against a 9.19 kBtu/ft²·yr limit**, a miss of about 3%. Net source
energy lands 203 kWh/yr over its limit, which a token amount of on-site PV would resolve. No amount of PV
can offset the heating-demand criterion.

So certification is within a few percent. Closing that last few percent means spending on the envelope well
past the point of diminishing returns, because the number is being driven by the geometry rather than by the
construction. The design team should understand that clearly before committing to the certification path.

### Resilience

Separately from the annual energy model, the home was stress-tested against the **Phius REVIVE** seven-day
outage protocol for both a summer heat wave and a winter storm. It passes both criteria with a very wide
margin, and no envelope or glazing changes are recommended on resilience grounds. See the
[Resilience](/resilience/) page for the full results.
