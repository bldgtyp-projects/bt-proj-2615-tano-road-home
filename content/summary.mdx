This report evaluates the energy performance, thermal comfort, resilience, and long-term durability of the proposed
Tano Road home under six design variants, ranging from a code-minimum baseline through the
**B.Public** panel packages to a full **Phius CORE 2024** configuration. The following pages document the
modeling assumptions, the results, and our recommendations.

### What we recommend

Use the [**B.Public**](https://www.bpublicprefab.com) R-52 wall panel with the R-59 roof panel
plus R-25 sub-slab insulation, U-0.22 windows, 1.0 ACH@50 airtightness, and a high-performance balanced ERV. **Do not pursue Phius
certification.**

### What the Passive House model shows

The **B.Public** panel system works well for this building and climate. Moving from code-minimum to the base
**B.Public-R-35 wall & B.Public-R-59 roof** package cuts total site energy roughly in half, from 50.6 to 26.1 kBtu/ft²·yr. 
Upgrading the walls to the **R-52 panel** takes site energy all the way down to **24.1 kBtu/ft²·yr**: an excellent 
building which uses less than **half** the energy of a code-compliant house on the same plan.

However, past that point the improvement in energy performance of potential upgrades becomes more modest. The 
**B.Public-R-80 roof** panel saves only 0.6 kBtu/ft²·yr, the smallest return of any upgrade tested here, and should be decided on panel 
price rather than on energy performance. The full **Phius CORE 2024** variant requires R-62 effective walls, an 
R-41 sub-slab assembly, U-0.15 windows, and 0.6 ACH50 to achieve certification level performance.

### Why we do not recommend Passive House certification

While Passive House certification *is* achievable in this case, it is very challenging and requires substantial upgrades to all 
of the envelope components over the 'recommended' package. The underlying cause is the site plan: the spread-out geometry and 
the limited winter solar-gain mean that the envelope has to compensate. More details and discussion can be found in the 
[geometry](/energy_model/#model-geometry) section.

### Resilience

Separately from the annual energy model, the home was stress-tested against the **Phius REVIVE** seven-day
outage protocol for both a summer heat wave and a winter storm. It passes both criteria with a very wide
margin, and no envelope or glazing changes are recommended on resilience grounds. See the
[Resilience](/resilience/) page for the full results.
