This report evaluates the energy performance, thermal comfort, resilience, and long-term durability of the proposed
Tano Road home under six design variants, ranging from a code-minimum baseline through the
**B.Public** panel packages to a full **Phius CORE 2024** configuration. The following pages document the
modeling assumptions, the results, and our recommendations.

### What the model shows

The B.Public panel system works well for this building and climate. Moving from the code-minimum baseline to the base
**R-35 wall / R-59 roof** package cuts total site energy roughly in half, from 50.6 to 26.7 kBtu/ft²·yr.
Upgrading to the **R-52 walls** and then the **R-80 roof** takes that to 24.0 kBtu/ft²·yr, and drops annual
CO<sub>2e</sub> emissions from 15.2 to 6.6 tons/year. This improvement results in an excellent building: comfortable,
durable, quiet, with very low energy consumption.

The full **Phius CORE 2024** variant goes further still, with site energy down to 17.5 kBtu/ft²·yr and only 4.7 tons
CO<sub>2e</sub>/year. That sits inside the 5-ton benchmark described in the
[CO2 Emissions](/energy_model/#co2-emissions) section. It gets there with R-62 effective walls, an R-41
sub-slab assembly, U-0.15 windows, 0.6 ACH50 airtightness, and a shortened, heavily insulated ventilation
duct run.

### Phius certification?

While Phius certification is possible, we do not necessarily recommend it for this project. Due to the spread-out nature 
of the floor plan and the limited solar gain, this home has higher heating demand than normal. This means that while it
is still possible to get to Phius level, it means more insulation than usual, and better windows than usual. Phius CORE 2024
should be evaluated and considered carefully before committing to it. 


### Resilience

Separately from the annual energy model, the home was stress-tested against the **Phius REVIVE** seven-day
outage protocol for both a summer heat wave and a winter storm. It passes both criteria with a very wide
margin, and no envelope or glazing changes are recommended on resilience grounds. See the
[Resilience](/resilience/) page for the full results.
