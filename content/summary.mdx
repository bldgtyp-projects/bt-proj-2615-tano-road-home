This report evaluates the energy performance, thermal comfort, resilience, and long-term durability of the proposed
Tano Road home under six design variants, ranging from a code-minimum baseline through the
**B.Public** panel packages to a full **Phius CORE 2024** configuration. The following pages document the
modeling assumptions, the results, and our recommendations.

### What we recommend

Build to the **+R-52 Walls** package: the **B.Public** R-52 wall panel (effective R-49.5), the R-59 roof panel
(effective R-58.5), an R-25 sub-slab assembly, U-0.22 windows, 1.0 ACH50, and a balanced ERV. Do not pursue Phius
certification.

### What the model shows

The B.Public panel system works well for this building and climate. Moving from code-minimum to the base
**R-35 wall / R-59 roof** package cuts total site energy roughly in half, from 50.6 to 26.1 kBtu/ft²·yr. Upgrading the
walls to the **R-52 panel** takes that to **24.1 kBtu/ft²·yr** and drops annual CO<sub>2e</sub> emissions from 15.2 to
6.6 tons/year: an excellent building, at less than half the energy use of a code-compliant house on the same plan.

Past that point the curve flattens hard. The **R-80 roof** panel saves a further 0.6 kBtu/ft²·yr, the smallest return
of any upgrade tested here, and should be decided on panel price rather than on energy. The full **Phius CORE 2024**
variant reaches 17.5 kBtu/ft²·yr and 4.7 tons CO<sub>2e</sub>/year, inside the 5-ton benchmark described in the
[CO2 Emissions](/energy_model/#co2-emissions) section, but needs R-62 effective walls, an R-41 sub-slab assembly,
U-0.15 windows, and 0.6 ACH50 to get there.

### Why we do not recommend certification

Certification is achievable here, but the Phius variant passes heating demand by only 3.1% (8.90 against
9.19 kBtu/ft²·yr), peak heating load by 5.0%, and net source energy by 2.4%. PV can buy back source-energy margin; the
other two are pure envelope, and the heating-demand figure additionally depends on a 2% thermal-bridge allowance not
yet backed by 2D calculation. The underlying cause is fixed by the site plan: the 4.4 enclosure-to-floor-area ratio and
the self-shaded south glass follow from the program and massing, so the envelope is compensating for geometry. That is
why the Phius package needs 3" of site-applied mineral wool over finished panels, double the sub-slab foam, and a
considerably more expensive window. The decision also cannot be deferred, since sub-slab depth and window plane are
both early, irreversible commitments.

Two consequences follow. All three B.Public variants are modeled at **U-0.22**, the level Passive House comfort
guidance recommends for this climate, so the recommended package clears the radiant-asymmetry threshold without the
U-0.15 product certification would require. And at 6.6 tons CO<sub>2e</sub>/year it sits above the 5-ton benchmark, so
**on-site PV**, roughly 3 kW, becomes the mechanism that closes the carbon gap.

### Resilience

Separately from the annual energy model, the home was stress-tested against the **Phius REVIVE** seven-day
outage protocol for both a summer heat wave and a winter storm. It passes both criteria with a very wide
margin, and no envelope or glazing changes are recommended on resilience grounds. See the
[Resilience](/resilience/) page for the full results.
