For this building, we recommend a high-performance ERV with better than 80% rated heat recovery and better than 60%
moisture recovery. The [Zehnder America ComfoAir Q600 ERV](https://zehnderamerica.com/products/ventilator/comfoair-q/)
would be a good selection and provide excellent indoor air quality while minimizing energy consumption and occupant
comfort issues. These units are outfitted with excellent air filtration, MERV 13 by default, which matters
for clean and healthy indoor air.

The **effective** heat recovery efficiency in the energy model runs lower than the unit's rated figure, because
it accounts for heat lost from the ducting that runs outside the thermal envelope. As modeled, that effective value is
**75%** for the B.Public variants and **78%** for the Phius CORE 2024 variant. The difference between those two numbers
comes entirely from shortening the exterior duct runs from roughly 16 ft to 8 ft each and doubling their insulation
from 1" to 2". That is a low-cost, high-return change and we recommend it whichever envelope package is
selected. Locate the ERV so that the outdoor-air and exhaust-air ducts are as short as physically possible.

### Casita zone control

The casita is a wing of the main house rather than a separate structure, and it is served by the same ERV as the rest
of the home. Following discussion with the team, the model now assumes the wing is used intermittently: guests stay
occasionally, and the rest of the year the casita ventilation **ramps down to 25% of design flow**. In airflow terms
that is 8 CFM supply to the casita bedroom and 16 CFM total extract from the casita bathroom and living/kitchen,
against 32 CFM each at high speed.

<div class="btwr-callout btwr-callout--critical">
  <span>
    The reduced casita flow is now a performance requirement: it is one of the two changes that brought the
    Phius CORE 2024 heating-demand result under its limit. The zone control described here has to be installed and
    commissioned for the modeled performance to be achieved.
  </span>
</div>

Deliver it with **zone control on the main unit** instead of a second ERV: a motorized damper on the casita
supply and extract branches, switched by an occupancy sensor or a simple manual boost, with the main unit stepping its
total airflow to match. That costs considerably less than a dedicated unit and avoids a second piece of equipment to
filter, service, and commission. A dedicated ERV only becomes the better answer if the casita is eventually intended to
operate as an independent dwelling with its own kitchen and its own schedule.

Two things to confirm during design development. The boost control needs to be obvious and reachable for guests, or
the wing will simply run stale. And the reduced-flow condition should be verified at commissioning alongside the
full-flow balance, since it is now a performance assumption rather than a convenience feature.

The values used in this analysis are assumed only. <Var k="narrative.mechanical.erv.manufacturer_name" /> should be
retained to fully design, specify, and balance the system after installation. This matters for proper operation and is
required for all high-performance buildings. The balancing service will normally come standard with
<Var k="narrative.mechanical.erv.manufacturer_name" /> packages, but this should be verified in this case.

#### System balancing

Prior to occupancy, the fresh-air system should be tested and balanced to ensure good air mixing and adequate supply to
all living spaces. The ERV vendor should provide this balancing as part of their services. Measured supply and extract
airflows must agree within 10% of each other.
