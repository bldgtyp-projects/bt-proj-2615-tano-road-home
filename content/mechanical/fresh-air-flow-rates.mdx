The fresh-air system should be sized around the room-by-room supply, transfer, and extract strategy rather than around a
single whole-building ventilation rate. The table below keeps the modeled airflow assumptions visible for design
coordination and commissioning.

As modeled, the system moves **260 CFM** at high speed and **200 CFM** at its normal operating (medium) speed, balanced
supply to extract. Supply is delivered to the living, dining, gym, office, bedrooms, and casita bedroom; extract is
drawn from the kitchen, all bathrooms and WCs, and the casita living/kitchen. Halls, closets, the pantry, the
mud/laundry room, and the entry act as transfer zones and receive no dedicated supply or extract.

The low-speed column applies to the casita alone. When that wing is unoccupied its three ventilated rooms drop to 25%
of design flow, which is **8 CFM** supply to the casita bedroom and **16 CFM** total extract from the casita bathroom
and living/kitchen. The rest of the house continues at its normal rate.

Two coordination notes follow from the plan:
- Duct runs are long. The wings are strung out, so this system carries considerably more duct than a compact house
would. Duct length and insulation quality directly affect the effective heat recovery efficiency, and they are one
of the differences between the B.Public and Phius CORE variants in the model.
- The casita ramp-down is now a performance assumption. It contributes to the Phius CORE
2024 heating-demand result, so the zone control that delivers it has to be installed and commissioned. See the
[Ventilation System Balancing](#ventilation-system-balancing) section.
