Taking into account the climate, orientation, and shading, in the results below we have assessed the average seasonal
(winter / summer) solar radiation falling upon the windows in the project. The radiation levels will vary by orientation
and, as can be observed, shading obstructions also have a strong effect on the final level of solar radiation any
individual window receives.

The home has <Var k="narrative.user_defined.window_area_total_ft2" /> ft² of glazing in total. How that area is
distributed, and how much useful winter heat each orientation actually delivers, is the core finding of this section:

*(Modeled gains shown for the recommended +R-52 Walls variant.)*

Two things stand out.

1. South glass is roughly six times as productive as east glass here. A square foot of
south glazing delivers 28.1 kWh over the heating season; a square foot of east glazing delivers 4.6. Yet east is the
largest single orientation on the building at 34% of total glazing area, while south is 23%. The glazing is
distributed almost the opposite of the way the climate would reward.
2. The south glass is also underperforming its own potential. The climate file shows an unobstructed south-facing
vertical surface in Santa Fe receiving about 13.7 kWh/ft² in January alone. After glazing losses, the south windows on
this building deliver well under what an unshaded south window would. The difference comes from the garage wing and the
opposing courtyard walls as described in the [Site Shading](#site-shading) section. In the
radiation study above, only the large slider bank shows meaningful winter gain.

### What this means for the results

Across the whole building, heating-season solar gains total roughly 8,500 kWh against roughly 27,500 kWh of envelope
and ventilation losses. A compact, well-oriented house of the same floor area in this climate would both lose
considerably less and gain considerably more.

This imbalance is the clearest reason the Phius CORE 2024 path is expensive here. That variant does meet the
heating-demand limit, at 8.90 against 9.19 kBtu/ft²·yr, but almost entirely on envelope performance rather than solar
harvest, which is why its specification is so heavy and its margin only 3.1%. The glazing recommendations below stand
on their own merits: they cost nothing and reduce heating demand whichever package is built.

### Recommendations

- Where glazing area is still being adjusted, move it south. Even modest reallocation from the east and north
  elevations to the south elevation improves the winter balance materially, at no change in total glass area, cost, or
  daylight.
- Reduce north glazing where it is not doing architectural work. North glass returns 6.7 kWh/ft² of gain against a
  full winter's worth of loss. A 5% to 10% reduction in north glazing area would improve the overall energy balance of
  the home.
- Keep south glass unshaded in winter. Where a portal or overhang covers south glazing, check whether it can be
  detailed to admit the low December sun while still blocking the high June sun. The two angles differ by about 47° at
  this latitude, which is usually enough geometry to work with.
