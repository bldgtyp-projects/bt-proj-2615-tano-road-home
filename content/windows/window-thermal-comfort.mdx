In any high-performance building, both the energy and occupant comfort impacts of the glazing must be carefully
assessed. For a building seeking any of the PHI or Phius certifications, a very stringent evaluation and quantification
of this thermal comfort impact must be executed. This comfort evaluation looks at both the overall occupant comfort as a
result of air-temperature and relative-humidity but also the localized thermal discomfort caused by cold-surfaces and
drafts at the glazing surface.

In particular, the localized discomfort which results from a radiant temperature asymmetry (a difference in the
temperature of the surfaces surrounding the body) must be assessed in order to evaluate the possibility of eliminating
perimeter heating. If the radiant temperature asymmetry exceeds a certain level, a compensating heat source may be
required in order to offset the potential discomfort. Typically, this would mean a radiator of some form, installed
beneath the window.

Many engineering reference standards suggest that ideal thermal comfort is found when there are temperature differences
of less than 7.6°F [4.2°K] between all the surfaces around a person’s body. The windows are important in this respect,
as this is where the coldest surface temperatures in the building will typically occur during the winter months.

### Window Recommendations

The window packages modeled in this report are set against that threshold:

- The **Improved** variant assumes an installed U-0.24 [R-4.2] window. That is a good window, but it sits slightly on
  the wrong side of the U-0.22 comfort threshold.
- All three **B.Public** variants, including the recommended **+R-52 Walls** package, assume an installed
  U-0.22 [R-4.5] window. This lands exactly on the comfort criterion for this climate's winter design conditions, and
  it is the reason the recommended package specifies this level rather than the marginally cheaper U-0.24 product.
  Meeting the threshold is what allows the design to proceed without planning for supplemental perimeter heat under
  the large glazed areas.
- The **Phius CORE 2024** variant assumes an installed U-0.15 [R-6.7] window, which clears the comfort criterion with
  substantial margin everywhere on the building. This very low value is driven by energy savings, not comfort.

Because the recommended package sits *at* the threshold rather than above it, the specified product matters: a window
testing slightly worse than U-0.22 once whole-window frame and spacer performance is counted would put the slider bank
and tall living-space glass back on the wrong side of the line.

If the project pursues formal certification, Phius evaluates comfort **window by window** rather than as a building
average, and also requires an interior-surface condensation check on both the glass and the frame. Both
checks would need to be run against the actual specified product once a manufacturer is selected. Whole-window
performance data is required, not center-of-glass, and the installation psi-value depends on how the window sits
relative to the insulation layer. That detail is worth resolving early on this project given the possible 3" of
exterior mineral wool discussed in the [Recommended Assemblies](/building_envelope/#recommended-assemblies) section.
