"Site Energy" represents the energy purchased by the building and delivered to the site by the utility. This is the most
typical energy use figure assessed when considering a site "Net-Zero" building energy balance, or when considering the
annual cost of energy for the building. This site-energy total is made up of 6 main groups: heating, cooling, hot-water,
lighting, electric equipment (appliances, electric-vehicles, etc), and pumps / fans.

In order to assess the performance of the home across a range of options, we have simulated six variants with
different energy efficiency measures (see the [Model Variants](#model-variants) section for all the details on the
specific variant inputs). All variants are modeled as all-electric.

The results follow the pattern we would expect for a heating-dominated, high-surface-area building. Heating is the
dominant end use in every case, and it is where nearly all of the savings come from. Total site energy falls
from 40,611 kWh/yr at code-minimum to 20,996 kWh/yr with the base B.Public package, 19,356 kWh/yr with the recommended
+R-52 Walls package, 18,886 kWh/yr with the full B.Public upgrade, and 14,034 kWh/yr in the Phius CORE 2024 configuration.

Two points stand out in the chart. The first move is the big one: going from code-minimum to the "Improved"
specification cuts site energy by 43%. Most of that comes from two changes that cost comparatively little, namely
going from 3.0 to 1.0 ACH50 and replacing extract-only bath fans with a balanced ERV. Auxiliary electricity alone
drops from 4,900 to 512 kWh/yr.

Returns then flatten quickly, and unevenly. Upgrading the walls from the R-35 to the R-52 panel saves
a small amount of energy; adding the R-80 roof panel saves even less. Both steps add almost the same effective
R-value, roughly R-16 at the walls and R-17 at the roof, but the wall step returns more than three times as much
energy, because the wall area is larger and the roof starts from an already-deep R-58.5. That asymmetry is why the
recommended package takes the wall upgrade and treats the roof upgrade as a cost question.

Everything unrelated to heating, cooling, and ventilation (lighting, appliances, plug loads, and hot water) stays
effectively identical across the variants at roughly 7,100 kWh/yr. In the recommended package those fixed loads are
already more than a third of total site energy, and in the Phius CORE case they are about half. Past a certain point
the envelope stops being the lever, and appliance selection, lighting design, and occupant behavior become the larger
share of the utility bill.
