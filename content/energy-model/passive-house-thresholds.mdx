In order to evaluate a building's performance, [total annual energy consumption](#site-energy) is key, as shown above.
However, in addition to this top line figure the 'Passive House' framework suggests that the building
should also meet additional heating and cooling annual energy demand performance targets. It is also
useful to compare the peak-heating and peak-cooling loads to the recommended limits for Passive House
buildings. While these limits are not required for certification in all cases, it is still good practice
to attempt to meet them where possible. Where the home fails to meet these targets is a clear indication
that improvements are possible.

For **Phius CORE 2024**, all four space-conditioning limits are hard pass/fail criteria, and they are calculated
specifically for this building by the Phius Criteria Calculator based on its enclosure area, floor area, occupant
density, and location. For this project they are:

| Criterion | Phius CORE 2024 limit |
| --- | --- |
| Annual heating demand | ≤ <Var k="narrative.certification.phius_hd_limit" /> kBtu/ft²·yr |
| Annual cooling demand (sensible + latent) | ≤ <Var k="narrative.certification.phius_cd_limit" /> kBtu/ft²·yr |
| Peak heating load | ≤ <Var k="narrative.certification.phius_hl_limit" /> Btu/hr·ft² |
| Peak cooling load (sensible) | ≤ <Var k="narrative.certification.phius_cl_limit" /> Btu/hr·ft² |

### Reading the results

Cooling is a non-issue here. Every variant except the code-minimum baseline passes both cooling criteria by a wide
margin. Even the code-minimum baseline uses only about 2.0 kBtu/ft²·yr against an 8.4 allowance, and the Phius CORE
variant about 1.1. Santa Fe's dry air and large day-to-night temperature swing do most of that work, and the model
shows no latent cooling demand at all.

Heating is the whole story. Annual heating demand runs from 49.7 kBtu/ft²·yr at code-minimum down to 18.2 with the
full B.Public package. That is an excellent result in absolute terms, and still roughly double the 9.2 limit. Only the
Phius CORE 2024 variant approaches it, at **9.45 kBtu/ft²·yr**, which is a 3% miss.

Peak heating load behaves differently. The Phius variant lands at 5.6 Btu/hr·ft² against a 5.7 limit, so it **passes**.
That combination, passing peak load while missing annual demand, is characteristic of a building with little useful
solar gain. Peak load is set by the design-day temperature, which the envelope handles well. Annual demand is set by
the whole heating season, where a compact, south-facing house would be harvesting free solar heat that this plan
cannot. See the [Winter Radiation](/windows/#winter-radiation) section for that analysis.

<div class="btwr-callout">
  <strong>Design implication</strong>
  <span>
    The remaining heating-demand gap cannot be closed by mechanical equipment, by PV, or by better controls. Only two
    things close it: further reducing envelope losses, or increasing useful winter solar gain. Since the massing and
    glazing layout are fixed, that means more insulation and better glass, which is exactly the part of the budget
    where each additional dollar buys the least.
  </span>
</div>
