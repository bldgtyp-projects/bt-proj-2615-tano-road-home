In order to evaluate a building's performance, [total annual energy consumption](#site-energy) is key, as shown above.
However, in addition to this top line figure the 'Passive House' framework suggests that the building
should also meet additional heating and cooling annual energy demand performance targets. It is also
useful to compare the peak-heating and peak-cooling loads to the recommended limits for Passive House
buildings. Where the home fails to meet these targets is a clear indication that improvements are possible.

For **Phius CORE 2024**, all four space-conditioning limits are hard pass/fail criteria, and they are calculated
specifically for this building by the Phius Criteria Calculator based on its enclosure area, floor area, occupant
density, and location. Shown below are results for these assessment metrics, for each of the tested variants.

### Results

Cooling is a non-issue here. Every variant except the code-minimum baseline passes both cooling criteria by a wide
margin. Even the code-minimum baseline uses only about 2.0 kBtu/ft²·yr of cooling against an 8.4 allowance, and the
Phius CORE variant about 1.1. Santa Fe's dry air and large day-to-night temperature swing do most of that work, and
the model shows no latent cooling demand at all.

Heating is where the variants separate. Annual heating demand ranges from 38,913 kWh/yr at code-minimum down to
14,60 kWh/yr with the recommended +R-52 Walls package and 13,915 kWh/yr with the full B.Public upgrade. Those are 
excellent results in absolute terms, but still roughly double the Passive House limit. Only the Phius CORE 2024 variant 
clears the limit, at 7,152 kWh/yr.
