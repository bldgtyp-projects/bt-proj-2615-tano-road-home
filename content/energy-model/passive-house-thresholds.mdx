In order to evaluate a building's performance, [total annual energy consumption](#site-energy) is key, as shown above.
However, in addition to this top line figure the 'Passive House' framework suggests that the building
should also meet additional heating and cooling annual energy demand performance targets. It is also
useful to compare the peak-heating and peak-cooling loads to the recommended limits for Passive House
buildings. While these limits are not required for certification in all cases, it is still good practice
to attempt to meet them where possible. Where the home fails to meet these targets is a clear indication
that improvements are possible.

For **Phius CORE 2024**, all four space-conditioning limits are hard pass/fail criteria, and they are calculated
specifically for this building by the Phius Criteria Calculator based on its enclosure area, floor area, occupant
density, and location. Shown below are results for these assessment metrics, for each of the tested variants.

### Reading the results

Cooling is a non-issue here. Every variant except the code-minimum baseline passes both cooling criteria by a wide
margin. Even the code-minimum baseline uses only about 2.0 kBtu/ft²·yr of cooling against an 8.4 allowance, and the
Phius CORE variant about 1.1. Santa Fe's dry air and large day-to-night temperature swing do most of that work, and
the model shows no latent cooling demand at all.

Heating is where the variants separate. Annual heating demand runs from 48.5 kBtu/ft²·yr at code-minimum down to 18.1
with the full B.Public package. That is an excellent result in absolute terms, and still roughly double the 9.2 limit.
Only the Phius CORE 2024 variant clears it, at **8.90 kBtu/ft²·yr**, a margin of 3.2%. Peak heating load follows the
same pattern, with the Phius variant at 5.42 Btu/hr·ft² against a 5.71 limit, a margin of 5.1%.

<div class="btwr-callout">
  <strong>Design implication</strong>
  <span>
    The heating-demand and peak-load results pass, with margins of 3.2% and 5.1%. Margins that size do not survive
    much slippage. Before the certification path is committed to, the slab edge, wall-to-roof, and panel-to-panel
    junctions should be modeled explicitly in 2D so the 2% thermal-bridge allowance is backed by calculation.
    The same caution applies to the window package: the modeled U-0.15 assembly needs to be matched by an
    actual specified product.
  </span>
