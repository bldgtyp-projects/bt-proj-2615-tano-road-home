Santa Fe sits at roughly 7,000 ft in ASHRAE climate zone <Var k="narrative.climate.ashrae_location_name" />. Its
climate is unusually favorable for a low-energy building in three specific ways.

It is sunny, and sunniest on the south face in winter. A south-facing vertical surface here receives about
13.7 kWh/ft² in January, against 5.5 on the east and 1.8 on the north. Peak south radiation occurs in
*November through February*, not in summer. This is an exceptional passive-solar resource by US standards, and it
is why the orientation and shading of the glazing matter so much on this project.

It is dry. There is effectively no latent cooling load, which removes dehumidification from the mechanical
problem entirely and makes summer comfort far easier to achieve.

The diurnal swing is large. Summer nights fall to roughly 52 °F even when afternoons reach into the high 90s,
which lets the building be flushed and re-cooled overnight. This contributes a great deal to the strong summer
resilience results.

The winter is real, however. The ASHRAE 99% winter design temperature is
<Var k="narrative.climate.ashrae_design_temps" />, and the modeled heating season drives nearly all of the
variation between the design variants in this report.

It should be noted that for the Passive House energy models, monthly average climate data are used and therefore may
appear different from the more typical ASHRAE hourly data shown in some other US Energy Modeling
programs. The monthly data is all derived from the same sources (local weather stations) as the
typical ASHRAE data however. The [Resilience](/resilience/) assessment uses full hourly data for its
outage simulations.
