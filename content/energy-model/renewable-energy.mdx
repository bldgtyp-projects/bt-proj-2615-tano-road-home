dek: "Size the on-site PV that would close the source-energy gap and reduce operating cost."

No solar PV is included in any of the variants modeled in this report. The site is unshaded, high in altitude, and
sits under one of the best solar resources in the country, so that is worth revisiting. It is the cheapest remaining
lever on this project by a wide margin.

Phius CORE 2024 sets its source-energy limit on a **net** basis, at
<Var k="narrative.certification.phius_nse_limit" /> kWh/person·yr, or 25,875 kWh/yr for the five-person occupancy
modeled here. On-site renewable generation is credited against that total. Two consequences follow directly.

The Phius CORE variant is nearly there already. It models at 26,078 kWh/yr of net source energy, only about
200 kWh/yr over the limit. At the modeled all-electric source factor of 1.8, roughly **115 kWh/yr** of PV production
closes that gap, which is less than the annual output of a single panel.

The +R-80 Roof variant would need about 3 kW. It models at 35,116 kWh/yr, about 9,240 kWh/yr over the limit, which
corresponds to roughly 5,100 kWh/yr of PV production. In Santa Fe that is a **3.0 to 3.5 kW** array, small by
residential standards.

<div class="btwr-callout">
  <strong>Important distinction</strong>
  <span>
    PV can satisfy the source-energy criterion. It cannot satisfy the annual heating-demand criterion. Those are
    separate pass/fail tests. Adding PV to the B.Public package would produce a home with the source-energy profile of
    a certified Passive House that still would not certify.
  </span>
</div>

Independent of certification, we recommend the design team carry PV in the scope for these reasons:

1. The roof area is there. A spread-out single-storey plan has a poor surface-to-floor-area ratio for heat loss,
   but the same geometry gives an unusually large, well-oriented roof for collection. This building's weakness for
   heating demand is its strength for generation.
2. Phius CORE requires PV-ready detailing regardless. Conduit, structural provision, and electrical panel space
   should be built in even if panels are deferred.
3. Battery and outage performance. The [Resilience](/resilience/) results assume no power at all. A modest PV and
   storage system would turn an already very safe outage performance into a comfortable one.

Array sizing, inverter selection, and interconnection with the local utility fall outside the scope of this report and
should be coordinated with a solar designer during design development.
