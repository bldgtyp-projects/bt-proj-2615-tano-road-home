No solar PV is included in any of the variants modeled in this report. The site is unshaded, high in altitude, and
sits under one of the best solar resources in the country, so it is worth being specific about what PV would and
would not change.

Because this report does not recommend pursuing certification, PV here is not a compliance instrument. It is the
cheapest remaining way to move the building's carbon and utility numbers, and two independent gaps land on almost the
same array size. On **carbon**, the recommended package emits 6.6 tons-CO<sub>2e</sub>/yr against the
<Var k="narrative.co2.target_tons" />-ton benchmark used in the [CO2 Emissions](#co2-emissions) section; closing that
1.6-ton gap takes roughly 4,800 kWh/yr of production. On **source energy**, Phius CORE 2024 sets a **net** limit of
<Var k="narrative.certification.phius_nse_limit" /> kWh/person·yr, or 25,875 kWh/yr for the occupancy modeled here;
the recommended package models at 34,841 kWh/yr, and at the all-electric source factor of 1.8 closing that gap takes
roughly 5,000 kWh/yr. Either way the answer is about a **3 kW** array, small by residential standards.

<div class="btwr-callout">
  <span>
    A 3 kW array does more for this building's operating carbon than any remaining envelope upgrade available to it.
    The step from the recommended package to the full B.Public package saves about 0.16 tons-CO<sub>2e</sub>/yr; the
    array saves roughly 1.6, ten times as much.
  </span>
</div>

We recommend the design team carry PV in the scope for these reasons:

1. The roof area is there. A spread-out single-storey plan has a poor surface-to-floor-area ratio for heat loss,
   but the same geometry gives an unusually large, well-oriented roof for collection.
2. It is the only remaining lever on operational carbon, as described above. Every envelope upgrade still available
   to this project returns a small fraction of what the array does.
3. Battery and outage performance. The [Resilience](/resilience/) results assume no power at all. A modest PV and
   storage system would turn an already very safe outage performance into a comfortable one.
4. PV-ready detailing costs almost nothing at this stage and a great deal later. Conduit, structural provision, and
   electrical panel space should be built in even if the panels themselves are deferred to a later phase.

Array sizing, inverter selection, and interconnection with the local utility fall outside the scope of this report and
should be coordinated with a solar designer during design development.
