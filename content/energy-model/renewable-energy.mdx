title: "Renewable Energy"
dek: "Assess on-site PV now that the source-energy limit is met without it."
---

No solar PV is included in any of the variants modeled in this report. The site is unshaded, high in altitude, and
sits under one of the best solar resources in the country, so it is worth being specific about what PV would and
would not change.

Phius CORE 2024 sets its source-energy limit on a **net** basis, at
<Var k="narrative.certification.phius_nse_limit" /> kWh/person·yr, or 25,875 kWh/yr for the five-person occupancy
modeled here. On-site renewable generation is credited against that total.

**The Phius CORE variant already meets that limit with no PV at all**, at 25,260 kWh/yr against the 25,875 allowance.
Certification does not depend on an array. The **+R-80 Roof** variant models at 34,725 kWh/yr, about 8,850 kWh/yr over
the limit. At the modeled all-electric source factor of 1.8, closing that gap would take roughly 4,900 kWh/yr of
production, which in Santa Fe is a **3.0 kW** array, small by residential standards.

<div class="btwr-callout">
  <span>
    The source-energy margin on the Phius variant is 2.4%, thinner than it looks. Source energy scales with everything
    the occupants plug in, and the fixed loads (lighting, appliances, plug loads, hot water) already account for about
    half of site energy. An additional freezer, a hot tub, or an EV charged at home would erase that margin. A modest
    PV array is the cheapest insurance against the model being right and the building still missing.
  </span>
</div>

Independent of certification, we recommend the design team carry PV in the scope for these reasons:

1. The roof area is there. A spread-out single-storey plan has a poor surface-to-floor-area ratio for heat loss,
   but the same geometry gives an unusually large, well-oriented roof for collection.
2. Phius CORE requires PV-ready detailing regardless. Conduit, structural provision, and electrical panel space
   should be built in even if panels are deferred.
3. Battery and outage performance. The [Resilience](/resilience/) results assume no power at all. A modest PV and
   storage system would turn an already very safe outage performance into a comfortable one.
4. Headroom against the source-energy limit, as described above.

Array sizing, inverter selection, and interconnection with the local utility fall outside the scope of this report and
should be coordinated with a solar designer during design development.
