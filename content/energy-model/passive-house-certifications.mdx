Meeting the modeled performance thresholds is necessary for certification but not sufficient. If the team elects to
pursue formal **<Var k="narrative.certification.target" />** certification, the following also apply:

- Airtightness testing. A measured blower-door result of no more than
  <Var k="narrative.certification.phius_cfm50_limit" /> CFM50 per ft² of gross enclosure area. For this building's
  <Var k="narrative.user_defined.envelope_area_ft2" /> ft² enclosure, that is a generous target. See the
  [Building Airtightness](/building_envelope/#building-airtightness) section.
- Window comfort and condensation. Every window must satisfy the Phius comfort criterion and an interior-surface
  condensation check on both glass and frame. See [Window Thermal Comfort](/windows/#window-thermal-comfort).
- Thermal-bridge modeling. Every junction with a psi-value above 0.006 Btu/hr·ft·°F must be individually modeled
  rather than covered by a percentage allowance. The Phius CORE variant in this report assumes a 5% allowance as a
  placeholder; certification would replace that with calculated details.
- Required project personnel: a Phius Certified Consultant (CPHC), a Phius Certified Rater/Verifier, and, for the
  builder co-requisite, a Phius Certified Builder (CPHB).
- DOE Zero Energy Ready Home PV-Ready checklists, required for all Phius CORE projects whether or not PV is
  installed.
- Certification fees and review rounds: design review, construction observation and testing, and final review, each
  with associated documentation and fees.

<div class="btwr-callout">
  <span>
    None of these are unreasonable, and none of them are the obstacle on this project. The obstacle is the annual
    heating-demand criterion described in the previous section. Everything listed above is achievable on this building
    as designed.
  </span>
</div>
